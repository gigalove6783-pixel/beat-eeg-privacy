Does a brain–computer interface reveal who you are?

A BCI turns imagined left- or right-hand movement into commands and often sends something off the device: a feature vector, a command, or both. We measured how well an eavesdropper can tell which of 9 people produced what was sent, and whether that can be reduced without hurting control.

Identity score = balanced accuracy of an attacker naming the right person out of 9. Chance is –; 1.00 means everyone is identified.

What this does not show

  • A 9-person closed set (BCI Competition IV 2a), not a population.
  • Arm control is a replay of recorded EEG, not closed-loop control with a person.
  • An attacker's failure is not anonymization.
  • Device compromise, identifying metadata and unobserved sensitive attributes are out of scope.
  • Replay processing time is not online latency.

Replay: steer the arm, watch the eavesdropper

Recorded session-E trials drive a one-joint arm: left −5°, right +5°, hold when the decoder's confidence is below τ. One episode is 20 trials. Meanwhile an independently trained attacker sees only what is transmitted and guesses who the user is from each group of k consecutive outputs. Every value shown is a stored result; nothing is re-computed from EEG.

    Method and integrity

    • Pre-registered seal

      Plan, configs, code and all session-T choices were committed before session E was evaluated: –.

    • Independent attackers

      Logistic regression, RBF SVM and MLP, retrained on frozen outputs. Training-time adversary heads are reported, never used as evidence.

    • Failures reported

      H1 (1 calibration run: Riemannian – vs CSP –, p = –) and H2 were not supported and are reported as results.

    • Disclosed deviation

      The sealed artifact-rejection rule held – of E trials (– for S–) versus – of T, reacting to cross-day drift. The primary analysis holds only non-finite trials; the sealed rule is still reported.

    • Replay checks

      Feature and command transmission gave identical commands in –/– settings. Median processing – per trial (laptop CPU, replay).
